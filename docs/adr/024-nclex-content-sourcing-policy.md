## Context

The NCLEX-RN epic initially considered adapting existing copyrighted test-prep material — reworded closely enough to avoid detection while still tracking a specific publisher's question content and structure.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Adapt copyrighted test-prep material.** Rejected. Reworded copyrighted content is still infringing regardless of how much it's altered; adapting it "enough to evade copyright issues" is an attempt to obscure infringement, not avoid it.
- **License content from a commercial publisher.** Viable in principle, but no licensing arrangement exists and pursuing one is out of scope for this epic. Can be revisited later if desired.
- **Original content authored against the public NCSBN test plan.** Selected.

## Decision

All NCLEX-RN course content is either (a) structured against NCSBN's publicly published test plan — category/subcategory definitions and weighting, which is public exam-format documentation, not copyrighted item content — or (b) written originally, referencing public clinical/nursing-practice guidelines.

## Rationale

The test-plan structure and NGN item-type taxonomy are published by NCSBN as public exam-format documentation, not proprietary item content — using them as a structural foundation carries no infringement risk. Original item-writing against that structure achieves full content coverage without any copyright exposure.

## Consequences

Content authoring carries the full cost of original item-writing — no shortcut via adaptation — but eliminates copyright risk entirely and removes any ambiguity for future contributors about what source material is permissible.
